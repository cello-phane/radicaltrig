Slope Accumulator

Slope accumulator

Walk along a curve in steps of h. At each step, split the secant slope into the tangent slope plus a remainder Q, store both as dangents, then rebuild the curve from the codes alone.

The split at one step

Teal segments are the secants between grid points. The dashed rust line is the tangent at step k. f₂ is the tangent's rise over h; f₁ is how far the curve ends up above it.

Euler plus accumulated Q

Following tangents only (rust, dashed) falls away from the curve. The shaded gap is exactly the running sum of h·Q. The teal line is rebuilt from the stored codes.

What gets stored

Top: Q per step (bars) and its decoded value (dots). Bottom: the difference angle as a dangent, dB, which is what is actually stored beside the tangent's dangent.

The code stream

Each row is one step. dt and dB are the two integers that get stored. Everything to their right is decoded from them: t = d/(1−|d|), then ms = (tt + tB)/(1 − tt·tB), then Q = ms − mt.

Reading it

Linear: every Q and every dB is exactly 0. A constant slope costs nothing after the first code.

Curved functions: Q is about f″(x)·h/2, so halve h and the codes dB halve too. With few bits, small dB values round to 0 or to a coarse step, and the decoded curve can drift away from the true one. More bits shrink the drift, which is the cost of compact storage.

Kink: at the corner the tangent is ambiguous (the page uses the right-hand slope). The one step that straddles the corner keeps a Q of up to the full slope jump however small h gets, while every other step stays exact. A remainder that does not shrink with h marks a non-differentiable point.
