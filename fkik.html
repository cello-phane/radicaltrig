RAU FK / IK

how a joint chain is described, moved and solved — with no arctangent anywhere in the loop

1Describe a point by distances: E = Point(A: r; B: α, C: β; D)

A link carries four reference points A, B, C, D that move rigidly with it. Any point E attached to the link is stored as three lengths, all in the same unit — not as x, y, z.
  • r = |AE|, the distance from A.
  • α and β are arc lengths centred at A: α = phaseB · |AB|, β = phaseC · |AC|, where each phase is the RAU angle between AE and AB (or AC).
  • D only picks the side of plane ABC. On the far side the arcs are the long way round: (4 − phase) · |AB|.
Forward (E → r, α, β)
phase = rauVec3AnglePhase(AB, AE)  ·  z = Ê·n picks the side
Inverse (r, α, β → E)
x = cos(phaseB)   y = (cos(phaseC) − x·cosγ) / sinγ   z = ±√(1−x²−y²)
E = A + r · (x·e₁ + y·e₂ + z·n)
schematic — the three magnitudes r, α, β share one distance unit
Needs A, B, C not collinear. When E lies almost exactly in plane ABC the short-arc / long-arc decision is a hard threshold.

2Two kinds of phase: raw from vectors, uniform for sliders

equal angle steps (blue ticks) land unevenly on the chord (amber ticks)
Move along the chord from (1,0) to (0,1) by a fraction w, then project the point radially onto the unit circle. That point is (cosθ, sinθ).
Raw phase — what two vectors give you
w = |u×v| / ( |u·v| + |u×v| )   (+ quadrant offset)
(cosθ, sinθ) = (1−w, w) / √((1−w)² + w²)
Uniform phase — proportional to angle
t ∈ [0,1) per quarter turn  →  w = warp(t)  (7-coefficient polynomial)
Slider: 1 RAU = 90°, 4 = full turn
  • A raw phase is not proportional to the angle (up to ~4° off), but rotating by it is exact — dot and cross already encode sin θ and cos θ.
  • So aiming and point construction stay raw. The warp is only paid for where the angle itself must be uniform, and the inverse warp only to print degrees.

4Inverse kinematics: CCD with raw phase

one CCD step: rotate everything past joint i so u swings onto v
Walk from the last joint back to the base. At each joint i:
  1. u = tip − joint    v = target − joint
  2. axis k = u×v / |u×v|    raw phase = rauVec3AnglePhase(u, v)
  3. rotate every joint beyond i about k by that raw phase (rodriguesRaw)
Why there is no arctangent
Rotating by the raw phase uses (1−w, w)/norm = (cosθ, sinθ) of the true vector angle — exact by construction. The angle is never turned into degrees to move the joint.
  • Repeat sweeps until the tip error stops shrinking. Demo chain (4 links): ~10 sweeps to ~1e-10.
  • u ∥ v: nothing to do. u antiparallel to v: pick any axis ⟂ u and use phase 2 (a half turn).
  • Out of reach: the chain stretches straight toward the target. Link lengths drift only at ~1e-15.

The descriptor is independent of the rotation scheme

Whether a joint moves A, B, C, D with a Rodrigues matrix or a quaternion, the same (r, α, β) and the same inverse construction recover E. Choosing a rotation backend and choosing this point description are separate decisions.
